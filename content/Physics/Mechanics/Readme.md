# Mechanics

## Overview
Mechanics models objects whose motion is predictable from position, velocity, forces, and constraints. It is relevant when quantum effects and relativistic speed are negligible: machines, orbits, structures, vehicles, and many engineering systems.

> **Authoritative sources**
> - [Landau & Lifshitz, *Mechanics* (Course of Theoretical Physics, Vol. 1)](https://www.physics.msu.ru/eng/ll/)

## History
- 1687: Newton gives laws of motion and universal gravitation.
- 1736: Euler develops rigid-body dynamics.
- 1788: Lagrange introduces coordinate-based analytical mechanics.
- 1830s: Hamilton gives variational formulations and phase-space methods.

<details><summary>Details</summary>

Kinematics provides the language (position, velocity, acceleration) without asking why motion occurs. Dynamics adds the causal layer: forces produce acceleration, and the special case of zero acceleration is static equilibrium. The Lagrangian and Hamiltonian reformulations of dynamics (generalized coordinates, Noether's theorem, phase space) are treated inside the Dynamics page. Oscillations and Waves extend the equilibrium analysis to periodic and propagating solutions. Rigid-Body Mechanics handles extended bodies that do not deform, while Continuum Mechanics handles bodies that do.
