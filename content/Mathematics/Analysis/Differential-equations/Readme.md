# Differential equations

Differential equations relate a function to its rates of change, and they are the standard language for dynamics: motion, growth, diffusion, oscillation, waves, and fields.

## Brief history

- 17th–18th century: Newton solves the two-body problem with an ODE; Euler and Lagrange develop the theory of equations arising from mechanics and variational principles.
- 19th century: Cauchy and Lipschitz prove existence and uniqueness theorems; Poincaré founds the qualitative theory of dynamical systems.
- 20th century: Sobolev, Lions, and others build the weak-solution and functional-analytic machinery that makes nonlinear PDEs tractable.

## Main branches

- **Ordinary differential equations (ODEs)**: one independent variable; existence and uniqueness (Picard–Lindelöf), linear systems, stability, and dynamical systems.
- **Partial differential equations (PDEs)**: several independent variables; the classical families (elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic) such as Laplace, heat, and wave equations.
- **Qualitative theory**: phase portraits, stability of equilibria, bifurcations, and chaos, where explicit formulas are impossible but long-term behavior is still decidable.
- **Numerical methods**: discretization schemes (finite differences, finite elements, Runge–Kutta) that compute approximate solutions with controlled error.
